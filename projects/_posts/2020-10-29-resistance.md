Evolutionary biologists often study adaptation under extreme conditions, such as high drug concentrations or severe heat stress. But what happens under mild stress, or when environmental conditions differ only slightly? Our lab has discovered that **even tiny environmental differences can change which mutations are adaptive**, sometimes turning beneficial mutations into harmful ones. We use massively parallel experimental evolution to track hundreds of thousands of yeast lineages, each marked with a unique DNA barcode. This allows us to detect adaptive mutations with even very small fitness benefits, measure the distribution of fitness effects (DFE), and identify the genetic changes driving adaptation. We use this approach to investigate how resistance evolves across different drug concentrations, how yeast adapts across subtle temperature gradients, and how other small changes in selective pressures influence evolutionary outcomes. Ultimately, to predict evolution, we need to understand the range of environmental conditions over which evolutionary outcomes remain consistent—and when even small differences change the course of adaptation.
