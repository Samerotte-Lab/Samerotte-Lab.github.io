Biologists often study cells by measuring thousands or millions of them at once and reporting the average. But how much important biology is hidden when we average across cells? Even genetically identical cells growing in the same environment can behave remarkably differently. More surprisingly, we have discovered that **relationships that appear highly predictable across populations can weaken, disappear, or even reverse when examined in individual cells.** For example, ribosome content closely tracks growth rate across populations but not across individual cells. Similarly, transcriptional programs associated with stress across populations are poor predictors of which individual cells were grown in stressful environments.

But single-cell biology offers much more than an opportunity to revisit established relationships. Our lab develops new experimental and computational approaches, using combinatorial barcoding, to explore the full diversity of cellular behavior, looking beyond averages and conventional cell clustering to investigate variation within and among cells. We treat this variation not simply as noise to be explained away, but as a source of new biological insight. By examining how cellular traits vary together, we aim to discover previously hidden relationships, identify regulatory mechanisms and modules, and understand how individual cells collectively give rise to population-level behavior. This work also complements our efforts to predict mutation effects and evolutionary outcomes by helping reveal the cellular processes underlying the patterns we observe.
